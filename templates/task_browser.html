{# Step 1 — who, and it comes first for two reasons. Choosing the client lights up that client's existing blocks on the timeline below, so the question "have I already logged time against them today?" is answered before you go looking for a gap. And it is the only control with a popup: at the top of the body its dropdown opens downward into three hundred pixels of timeline, all inside the scroll container, so Choices' own absolute positioning works exactly as the library intends. Sitting last, it opened into the panel edge and was clipped to about two rows. #}
{# Step 2 — when. The chips are the way in and the timeline is context until one is pressed: the dialog opens with nothing selected and no draft on the timeline, because a form that opens with times already in it invites agreement rather than a decision. Once a range exists it becomes a draggable item, and the line under the timeline states it in words. The two pickers behind "Edit" are the escape hatch for an exact minute or a time outside the drawn day. #}
{# The way in. The longest few free stretches, as buttons — nothing is filled in until one is pressed, or the times are typed below. #}
